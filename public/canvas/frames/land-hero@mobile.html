How to pack your belongings

You pack everything yourself before pickup, furniture included. Each piece is then priced by its actual weight or its volumetric weight, whichever is higher.

  • Suitcases & bags

    Strong enough for what's inside

    Lock every zip, and secure any loose straps and handles.

  • Boxes

    Double-wall, up to 25 kg

    Pack them full and tape every side. Thin reused boxes, like drinks cartons, aren't recommended.

  • Special packaging

    Made for the piece

    A hard case for instruments, a crate or wrapping for furniture. Give us its exact dimensions when you ask for a price.

If something arrives damaged and wasn't packed to this guide, it can't be claimed at the value you declared.

How to pack furniture, step by stepSix steps, from emptying the drawers to labelling every piece.

Like everything else, furniture is packed by you. It ships where our carrier partner offers it, and we confirm that before you start.

  1. 01

    Get it ready

    Empty every drawer and shelf (what was inside goes on the packing list), clean and dry it, and photograph it from a few sides.

  2. 02

    Take it apart

    Remove legs, doors, shelves and drawers where you can. Bag and label the screws and fittings and send them in the same shipment; spares go on the packing list as their own item.

  3. 03

    Protect it

    Cover it in plastic, cloth or paper (cloth or paper first on leather and finished wood), add cardboard corner guards, wrap it in bubble wrap, foam or moving blankets, and seal it with stretch wrap.

  4. 04

    Pack it

    Small and medium pieces go in double-wall boxes, the gaps filled. Large or heavy pieces go in a wooden crate, heat-treated and stamped ISPM 15, braced so nothing moves.

  5. 05

    Measure and weigh it

    Measure and weigh every packed piece and send us the numbers. Each piece is charged on the higher of its actual or volumetric weight.

  6. 06

    Label it

    Number every piece (1/5, 2/5…), stick our shipping label on each, add Fragile or This Side Up where it helps, list its contents, value and condition, and photograph them all.

FAQ

Common questions before you ship

Still have a question? Ask us on WhatsApp

How do I book?

Check your rate first: pick where you're moving from and to and roughly what it weighs, and see an estimate straight away. Choose a shipping option, answer five quick questions about your move, and add your details. You'll get a booking summary by email. Our team then reviews your packing list and sends your quotation.

What can I ship?

Almost any personal belonging: clothes and personal items, books, home and kitchen things, and sports and hobby gear, packed in suitcases, boxes or special packaging like a hard case or a crate. Everything travels packed by you, furniture included, to our packing guide. Electronics, food, medicine, supplements, cosmetics and perfume need a check with our team first.

What can't I ship?

Alcohol, cigarettes and vapes, weapons and explosives, anything flammable, cash, jewellery, plants and motor vehicles. Indonesia's import bans apply on every route too, including used clothing sent in bulk. Electronics, food, medicine, supplements, cosmetics and perfume need a check with us first, since the rules depend on the item and the route. Not sure about something? Ask us before you pack it.

Do you pack my belongings?

No. You pack everything yourself before pickup, furniture included, following our packing guide. We don't pack or repack, at your door or at our warehouse. If something arrives damaged and wasn't packed to the guide, it can't be claimed at the value you declared.

Can I ship furniture?

Yes, where our carrier partner offers it. Like everything else, you pack it yourself: emptied, taken apart where you can, wrapped, and in a double-wall box or a wooden crate heat-treated and stamped ISPM 15. As relocation goods, furniture is duty-free too.

What documents will I need?

It depends on how your things clear customs. We review your booking first, then recommend the way that suits you best and ask for its documents. The choice is always yours. Every way needs a commercial invoice/packing list. On top of that, here's what each way needs. As relocation goods: your passport, your work or study ITAS (valid 12 months or more) with your work approval or study permit, and your signature on a power of attorney we prepare, so our trusted partners can declare your things to customs on your behalf. As passenger goods sent separately: your passport, and your arrival date and flight details. As a personal shipment: your passport or NPWP. Customs may occasionally ask for additional documents. We'll let you know if they do.

What's a commercial invoice/packing list?

One list of everything you're sending, package by package, with how many of each item and what each is worth, plus who's sending it and who's receiving it. The value is what each item is worth today, not what you paid, and no receipts are needed. Despite the name, it isn't a bill and you're not selling anything: customs uses it to see what's inside your shipment. You create it yourself on Rimkirim's packing list page.

How is the price worked out?

Chargeable weight × the rate per kilo for your route. Each piece's chargeable weight is the higher of its actual weight and its volumetric weight (length × width × height in cm ÷ 5000). A piece that's very heavy, oversized or not in a plain cardboard box adds a handling surcharge. The rate you see when you check is an estimate, before surcharges. Your final price is on your invoice once your packages reach our warehouse in Indonesia.

Do I pay import duty or tax?

It depends on the route. As relocation goods, with a work or study ITAS of 12 months or more, your household things are duty-free with no value limit. As passenger goods sent separately, the first USD 500 per person per arrival is free, and the amount above it pays 10% duty plus VAT. As a personal shipment there's no duty-free allowance: up to USD 1,500, flat rates from 0% (printed books) to 25% (clothes, bags and shoes), plus VAT, and without an NPWP some items carry a higher income tax rate. Our price covers shipping; any duties and taxes are set by Indonesian Customs and charged separately. You don't need to choose a way now: when we review your booking, we'll point out the one that saves you the most, and the choice is yours. From your packing list, we tell you what's likely to be taxed, so you can leave it out if you'd rather.

Can my packages be opened?

Yes. Customs can physically inspect any shipment coming into Indonesia: the package is opened and what's inside is checked against its documents, for type, quantity, condition and value. Not every shipment is opened, but no one can rule it out, not us and not the courier, because customs picks shipments one by one, on risk and partly at random. A shipment that went through unopened before may be opened next time. Being opened doesn't mean anything's wrong, and it isn't what sets your duty and tax, which are worked out separately.

What makes an inspection more likely?

Customs weighs each shipment's documents, the kind of goods, the importer's and the goods' profiles and the X-ray, and picks some at random. It's judged shipment by shipment, not person by person. A shipment is more likely to be opened when:

  • what's inside, its type or its value isn't clear
  • the count or kind of goods doesn't seem to match the documents or the X-ray
  • it may hold prohibited or restricted goods
  • it holds goods that are always checked, such as animal and plant products, medicines, or goods that aren't personal

So list what's in each package clearly, with how many and what it's worth. Restricted goods aren't always opened, but their permits are always checked on paper.

What happens if a package is inspected?

Staff from the courier (FedEx, DHL or another) open it while a customs officer watches, check what's inside against the documents, including any permits, and close it again. If customs needs more information, we'll pass the question on to you: reply within 5 working days, or customs sets the rate and value from what it already has. Then one of three things happens:

  • Everything matches and nothing is due: it's released.
  • Duty or tax is due, or the value is set again: it's released once that's paid. If you disagree with the amount, ask for a written explanation before you pay, since paying can count as accepting it.
  • Restricted goods without their permit: get the permit, release the rest without them, or send them back. Prohibited goods can't be released at all, so they're sent back or destroyed.

An inspection adds time, and can add storage fees, which are billed to you. If anything is damaged in the process, you can claim for it, as long as your claim meets our claim requirements. Pack each package so it can be opened and closed again.
