If you're an Indonesian citizen returning home after living abroad, this guide explains exactly how customs treats the belongings you ship — which scheme you fall under, what duty relief you get, what documents you need, what you can't bring, and how clearance works. Written in plain English, with the governing regulations cited so you can verify every point.

## How Indonesia Classifies Your Belongings

The single most important thing to understand: Indonesia sorts incoming personal belongings into two customs schemes, and which one applies decides your documents, your duty relief, your value limits, and how long your goods can arrive before or after you.

**Barang Pindahan ("moved belongings")** — for Indonesian citizens (WNI) returning home for the long term after at least one year abroad.

**Barang Penumpang ("passenger's belongings")** — for shorter stays, or when the Pindahan criteria aren't met.

This guide is for Indonesian citizens (WNI) returning home. If you're a foreign national relocating to Indonesia, the schemes work similarly but the documents differ (VITAS, ITAS, RPTKA instead of an embassy letter).

One rule underpins everything: personal belongings import is only valid when the sender and the receiver are the same person — you are shipping your own belongings to yourself. A relative shipping goods to you, or an employer shipping to an employee, falls under different customs rules (gifts or commercial goods), not the personal belongings schemes described here.

One more thing worth knowing early: your scheme is set by the customs declaration filed on your behalf — not by how the goods physically travel. The same air shipment can be declared as Pindahan or Penumpang depending on your eligibility. Your shipping partner helps you choose before anything is filed.

## Which Scheme Fits You

Two questions decide it: how long you've been abroad, and whether you meet the Pindahan threshold.

| You are… | Scheme | Key condition | Value limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| WNI, abroad ≥ 1 year, returning home | Barang Pindahan | Continuous stay abroad of at least one year | No fixed maximum (reasonable personal quantity) |
| WNI, abroad < 1 year | Barang Penumpang | Ineligible for Pindahan | FOB USD 500 duty-free |

"Reasonable personal quantity" isn't a number — it's a judgement the customs officer makes based on your profile (how long you were away, the type and amount of goods). Shipping multiples of new electronics or commercial volumes of one item invites scrutiny.

## Duty-Free Relief and PMK 25/2025

Barang Pindahan is governed by PMK 25/2025 — Ministry of Finance Regulation No. 25 of 2025 on customs provisions for imports of transferred goods. It took effect on 27 June 2025, replacing the older PMK 28/2008, and it defines eligibility, the 90-day arrival window, and the special import-declaration route (PIBK) used for relocation goods.

The relief is generous — but it is not unlimited. The regulation explicitly excludes several categories from the duty-free facility:

| Excluded from duty-free relief | Legal basis |
|---|---|
| Motor vehicles (cars, motorcycles, similar) | PMK 25/2025 Pasal 2 ayat (4) huruf a |
| Water & air vehicles (boats, aircraft) | Pasal 2 ayat (4) huruf b |
| Spare parts & components of those vehicles | Pasal 2 ayat (4) huruf c |
| Excisable goods (tobacco, alcohol) | Pasal 2 ayat (4) huruf d |
| Goods in quantities unreasonable as moved belongings | Pasal 2 ayat (4) huruf e |

Important distinction: "excluded from the scheme" does not mean "banned from Indonesia." These items simply can't ride in under the duty-free relocation relief — they'd need a different customs route (and, for vehicles, usually full duties). If your shipment contains any of them, they must be separated out and handled differently.

There's also a layer above the duty rules: even with duty relief, your goods must still satisfy Indonesia's lartas (prohibition & restriction) framework — the permit and standards rules set by other ministries. As PMK 25/2025 Pasal 2 ayat (3) puts it, duty exemption does not override lartas. In practice, though, a key trade-side carve-out clears most ordinary household goods — which brings us to what you can and can't bring.

## Documents You'll Need

The document set depends on your scheme. Here are the practical checklists. (Your shipping partner confirms the exact current list before your airwaybill is created — customs requirements are periodically updated.)

**Barang Pindahan (Indonesian citizen returning home)**

- Passport copy — name must match every other document exactly
- Commercial Invoice / Packing List, itemised per box
- Return flight e-ticket (proof of arrival within the 90-day window)
- Proof of ≥ 1 year abroad: employment letter / Certificate of Employment, or for students a university enrolment / completion letter (CAS), showing start and end dates
- Electronic NPWP (validated) — or KTP if you have no NPWP
- Surat Keterangan Pindah (SKP) from your KBRI/KJRI
- Photo documentation of each box's dimensions and weight

**Barang Penumpang (shorter stay)**

- Passport copy
- Commercial Invoice / Packing List
- Return flight e-ticket
- Proof of arrival in Indonesia (boarding pass, arrival card, passport stamp, or statement letter)
- Electronic NPWP (validated) — or KTP if none

No SKP needed for Barang Penumpang. The embassy letter and the proof-of-residence-abroad letter are only required for the Pindahan scheme.

## The SKP — Your Most Important Document (WNI Pindahan)

The Surat Keterangan Pindah (sometimes Surat Keterangan Barang Pindahan) is the official letter from your Indonesian Embassy or Consulate (KBRI/KJRI) confirming your permanent return and listing your moved belongings. For WNI relocation goods, it's the document customs cares about most.

Two things to plan around:

**Embassy processing can be slow.** The SKP can usually be submitted later — at the latest when your shipment reaches the import transit warehouse — so your goods can ship while the letter is being issued. But start the application early.

**The list must match.** The SKP includes a Daftar Barang Pindahan (list of moved belongings). The items and quantities should line up with your packing list. If the declared values differ — often because the embassy converts currency — a short supplementary statement letter (Surat Pernyataan) resolves the discrepancy.

## What You Cannot Bring (and What Passes Cleanly)

This is where most surprises happen. The rule that saves the day for ordinary belongings is Permendag 16/2025 Pasal 42 (as amended by Permendag 37/2025) — a non-commercial import channel that exempts relocation goods from the heavy trade-side machinery (import licences, technical verification, port restrictions). Relocation goods are explicitly named in this channel, and it expressly allows goods in used condition.

Why your used clothes, furniture, and everyday electronics clear: they ride the Pasal 42 non-commercial channel, they're not on its hard-blocked list, and they're in personal quantities. Legal basis: Permendag 16/2025 Pasal 42 ayat (1), (2), (4) huruf f, and (7), as amended by Permendag 37/2025.

But that channel has a hard block list — 11 categories that cannot come through it no matter what. The ones that actually affect relocations:

| Blocked category | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Cooling-system goods | Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners — blocked, even used. Leave them; buy in Indonesia. |
| Electronic cooling-system goods | Same treatment as above. |
| HFC refrigerant gases | Not importable through this channel. |
| Used (loose) lithium batteries | Loose used batteries blocked. Note: this does not block a device that simply contains a battery. |
| Explosives, hazardous materials, ozone-depleting substances, rough diamonds, non-pharma precursors, nitrocellulose, non-B3 waste | All blocked — rarely relevant to a household move. |

And separately, for relocation and personal-shipment goods, motor vehicles cannot be imported through this channel (Permendag 16/2025 Pasal 42 ayat (10)), reinforcing the PMK 25/2025 exclusion above.

Beyond customs, your courier has its own no-fly list. Air carriers won't accept liquids (perfume, alcohol, cleaning fluids), devices with non-removable batteries, sharp weapons, cigarettes/vapes, flammable goods, liquid or meltable stationery (markers, candles), money and jewellery, creams/lotions/oils, prescription medication, and food containing meat, dairy, vegetables, or fruit. These are shipping restrictions, separate from the customs rules above.

## Limits on Cosmetics, Medicine, and Food

Cosmetics, medicines, supplements, and processed food are regulated by Indonesia's food-and-drug authority, BPOM. Normally these require registration to import — but there's a personal-use channel (under PBPOM 27/2022, as amended by PBPOM 28/2023) that lets you bring reasonable quantities for yourself, without registration, declared on a simple form at the border. The per-person ceilings:

| Item | Personal-use ceiling |
|---|---|
| Cosmetics | Max 20 pieces per person |
| Prescription medicine (obat keras) | Up to 90 days' supply, per doctor's prescription |
| Over-the-counter medicine (without prescription) | Per dosage form: 30 solid (tablet/capsule), 3 semisolid (cream/gel), 3 liquid, 3 aerosol |
| Herbal remedies / quasi-drugs / health supplements | Max 5 items each per person |
| Processed food (non-alcoholic) | Max 5 kg per person |

**Worked example — 40 lipsticks:** a customer relocating from London packs 40 lipsticks. They clear the trade-side rules fine, but BPOM's cosmetics ceiling is 20 per person. Result: reduce to 20 to clear cleanly. The same logic applies to any cosmetic over the cap.

For relocation (Pindahan) goods, remember the reasonableness test still sits on top: hitting the BPOM ceiling doesn't guarantee clearance if the quantity doesn't fit your story (20 lipsticks after a three-week trip will draw questions).

## The Import Process, Step by Step

All personal-belongings imports clear through the PIBK route (Pemberitahuan Impor Barang Khusus — a special import declaration). What happens:

1. The customs declaration (PIBK) is submitted electronically.
2. Customs reviews it for eligibility, document completeness, and consistency.
3. Your goods undergo physical inspection at the port — you or a representative should be present.
4. The officer assesses whether the type and quantity are reasonable for your profile.
5. If approved, a payment document is issued (if anything is owed); once settled, the SPPB (customs clearance approval) is generated and your goods are released.

**Shipment limits:** generally one sea shipment and one air shipment per person qualify for duty-free treatment. A second sea batch must arrive within 3 months of the first to stay eligible.

## Timing and Arrival Windows

Your goods don't have to travel with you, but they do have to arrive within a window tied to your own arrival:

| Scheme | Arrival window for your goods |
|---|---|
| Barang Pindahan | Up to 90 days before or after you arrive in Indonesia — PMK 25/2025 Pasal 4 ayat (1) huruf c |
| Barang Penumpang | Tied to your arrival; confirm the current window with your shipping partner before booking |

If your goods will arrive before you (a common WNI Pindahan situation — say your lease ended early), a short request letter called an SP3BP explains the timeline to customs and keeps you inside the 90-day rule. You write and sign it; your shipping partner can help draft it.

On top of the arrival window, build in transit time (air freight from Europe to Indonesia is roughly 4–8 days) plus 4–10 days for clearance, which varies with customs workload.

## Costs and Taxes

If you qualify for Barang Pindahan and your goods are reasonable personal belongings, the relocation relief means no import duty on the household goods themselves.

For Barang Penumpang, the duty-free allowance is FOB USD 500 per person per arrival (confirmed under PMK 34/2025 Pasal 12 ayat (1), amending PMK 203/2017). Value above USD 500 is charged a flat 10% import duty on the excess, plus import taxes (VAT, and income tax depending on your NPWP status).

Separately from customs, expect shipping-side charges from your logistics provider — freight (by chargeable weight), any handling surcharges, and temporary warehouse fees during inspection. Those are commercial costs, not government duties; your provider should set them out before you book.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can my belongings arrive before I do?** Yes — under Barang Pindahan, up to 90 days before your arrival. If they arrive ahead of you, an SP3BP letter explains the timeline to customs.

**Can a family member ship belongings to me in Indonesia?** Not under these schemes. Personal belongings import requires the sender and receiver to be the same person. A relative shipping to you is treated as a gift or commercial import, with different rules and duties.

**Can I bring my refrigerator or air conditioner?** No — cooling-system goods are on the hard-blocked list, even when used. Plan to buy these in Indonesia.

**Do I need an SKP if I was only abroad for eight months?** No — under a year abroad means you're in Barang Penumpang, which doesn't require an SKP. But you also get the USD 500 allowance rather than the fuller relocation relief.

**Are my used clothes and furniture a problem?** No. Used household goods are expressly allowed through the non-commercial channel; they're not "second-hand goods" in the restricted sense, which refers to used industrial/capital goods.
