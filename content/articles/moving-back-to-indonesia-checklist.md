A clear checklist for moving back to Indonesia can make the whole process of returning home a lot more organized, especially if you've been living abroad for years and have accumulated more belongings than you realize. Without proper preparation, the move can feel a lot more complicated and take longer than expected.

Many people planning to move to Indonesia focus only on their travel documents. But there's a lot more to prepare: from sorting your belongings and choosing a shipping method, to setting a budget and making sure your goods and paperwork are ready before shipping.

To make your move easier to prepare for, here's a checklist you can use as a guide before shipping your belongings from abroad to Indonesia.

## Why Moving From Abroad Takes Different Preparation

An international move isn't the same as a regular local move. Beyond the greater distance, there's cross-border shipping, customs requirements, and delivery estimates to factor in.

Shipping time also doesn't just depend on the journey from your country of origin to Indonesia. Your goods still need to go through customs clearance before being forwarded to their final destination. For a fuller picture of what that timeline looks like, see [how long does shipping take to Indonesia](/articles/how-long-does-shipping-take-to-indonesia).

That's why the earlier you start preparing, the more time you'll have to choose a shipping method, get your documents ready, and plan around any potential hiccups.

## Moving Back to Indonesia Checklist: 6 Things to Prepare

### 1. Understand Relocation Goods and Customs Requirements

One of the most important parts of an international move is understanding customs requirements around relocation goods.

To qualify for the applicable relocation goods facility, make sure you meet the requirements and have the necessary documents ready, including a Certificate of Relocation (Surat Keterangan Pindah / SKP) from the Indonesian Embassy or Consulate as required.

The SKP lists the relocation goods you're applying to bring in under the import duty and tax exemption facility, and what you actually ship needs to match that list and meet the applicable requirements. Keep in mind that not everything automatically qualifies. Make sure you understand the requirements for relocation goods and accurately declare the contents of your shipment before it goes out. For the full breakdown of requirements and documents, see [the complete customs guide for personal belongings](/articles/indonesia-customs-guide-personal-belongings).

### 2. Build a Moving Timeline

Don't wait until the last few days before your departure to sort out shipping. Build a timeline around your moving-out date or your flight to Indonesia.

Ideally, start preparing and packing your belongings around 14 days before departure, then book your pickup somewhere between 10 and 5 days out. That leaves you some buffer time in case your pickup schedule shifts, something comes up during shipping, or your goods need extra time at customs.

Also keep an eye on business days and holiday periods in both your country of origin and Indonesia. Avoid scheduling pickup too close to a weekend or public holiday, since logistics providers may operate on adjusted schedules during those times. Just as important: make sure your shipment details, destination address, and recipient information are all finalized before pickup. Changing this information after shipping has started can affect the administrative and customs clearance process.

### 3. Choose the Right Shipping Method

Once you know what you're bringing, decide on the shipping method that best fits the quantity, weight, volume, and needs of your shipment.

For a limited amount of belongings, courier or parcel services can work well. For a relocation shipment made up of several boxes or with greater weight and volume, cargo tends to be the more suitable option. If you're still not sure which one fits your situation, the full comparison is in [cargo vs courier for moving to Indonesia](/articles/cargo-vs-courier-for-moving-to-indonesia).

You'll also want to factor in speed and cost. Economy service generally takes longer but costs less, while Priority gets your shipment there faster at a higher price.

There's no single best method for everyone. The right choice depends on what you're shipping and how quickly you'll need it once it arrives in Indonesia.

### 4. Set a Budget and a Buffer

The cost of an international move isn't just the shipping fee. There are other components to factor in from the start: packing materials, add-on services, temporary storage if needed, and other costs that can come up along the way.

It's worth putting together a full cost estimate before settling on a shipping method. Compare a few service options and make sure you understand exactly what's included in each price.

Setting aside a buffer can also help absorb any extra costs that come up beyond your initial estimate.

### 5. Sort Your Belongings Before Packing

Before figuring out how to ship anything, decide what you actually want to bring back with you.

You can split your belongings into a few categories: things you definitely need to bring, items that still have value and could be sold or given away, and things better left behind since they're easy to find again in Indonesia.

Don't forget to think through items with sentimental value. Even if they're not worth much, some belongings become part of your story from living abroad. For anything you're not bringing, you can sell it, pass it on to a friend, or donate it.

Sorting early makes it much clearer how much you'll actually be shipping, which lets you estimate your packing needs and shipping costs more accurately.

### 6. Make Sure Packing and Documents Are Ready

Once your belongings are sorted, make sure everything is packed securely before pickup.

Use sturdy boxes sized appropriately for what's inside. For fragile or breakable items, use bubble wrap, foam, or other protective material. Avoid leaving empty space inside boxes so items don't shift around during transit.

It's also worth documenting the condition of your belongings before and during packing. Take photos of items before they're packed, once they're arranged in the box, and of the sealed package ready for pickup. This documentation can serve as evidence if you ever need to file a claim.

Beyond packing, make sure all your shipping documents are complete and your shipment details have been declared accurately. Any mismatch or last-minute changes between your documents and your actual shipment can affect the customs clearance process.

## Your Moving Back to Indonesia Checklist, Summarized

Moving back to Indonesia isn't just about bringing back as much as you can. By sorting your belongings early, understanding customs requirements, choosing the right shipping method, setting a budget, packing securely, and building a realistic timeline, your return can be a lot more planned out and a lot less stressful.

If you're still unsure about the best way to ship your belongings from abroad to Indonesia, don't hesitate to look into your options and talk to a shipping provider before deciding.
