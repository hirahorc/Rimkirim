Every shipment has different needs. Some prioritize cost, while others need their belongings to arrive faster. That's why shipping providers typically offer Economy and Priority options, with the main difference being estimated delivery time and cost.

*Note: Economy and Priority as covered in this article refer to **air freight** shipping options.*

## Economy (Air Freight): More Affordable, Takes Longer

If you're not in a rush and want to keep shipping costs down, Economy can be a good option. This service has an estimated shipping time of around 4–8 business days, not including the time needed for customs clearance.

Besides the international shipping process itself, goods entering Indonesia also need to go through a customs inspection. Customs clearance can take around 4–10 business days, so the total time until your goods reach their destination can end up longer than the initial shipping estimate.

The main advantage of Economy is a more affordable cost, making it suitable for shipments without high urgency. You can save on shipping costs by giving your goods more flexible time to arrive in Indonesia. For a full breakdown of what goes into the cost, see [what it costs to ship your belongings back to Indonesia](/articles/how-rimkirim-prices-your-shipment).

## Priority (Air Freight): Faster, at a Higher Cost

If you need your goods to arrive sooner, Priority can be the better option. This service has an estimated shipping time of around 3–5 business days, not including customs clearance time.

With a shorter shipping time than Economy, Priority suits more time-sensitive needs, or situations where you want your goods to reach Indonesia as soon as possible. That speed, of course, comes with a higher shipping cost.

Keep in mind that neither the Economy nor Priority estimate includes the customs clearance process, so actual delivery time can differ from the shipping estimate alone. Shipping costs also generally don't include additional charges such as import duty or tax under applicable regulations.

## What Affects Shipping Time

### 1. Type of Shipping Service

Your choice of service affects the shipping estimate. Economy generally takes longer than Priority, since each service has a different priority level and operational process.

### 2. Origin and Destination Country

Distance, shipping routes, and the availability of transport connections from the country of origin to Indonesia can affect how long your shipment takes. In some cases, goods also need to transit through another country before continuing on to Indonesia.

### 3. Public Holidays and Peak Season

National holidays in either the origin country or Indonesia can affect shipping schedules. During certain periods, logistics providers may adjust operations or see a spike in shipping volume.

For example, shipments from the United Kingdom around a Bank Holiday, or from Japan during Golden Week, can take longer due to increased operational activity and shipping volume. Because of this, it's worth checking the holiday calendar in both the origin country and Indonesia when planning your shipping timeline, especially if your goods need to arrive by a specific date.

### 4. The Customs Clearance Process

Once your goods reach Indonesia, they need to go through customs clearance before being forwarded to their destination. Inspection times can vary from one shipment to another, depending on queue volume, document completeness, and the goods being shipped. To help this process go more smoothly, make sure your shipping documents are complete and the contents of your shipment have been accurately declared from the start.

For those shipping relocation goods specifically, a Certificate of Relocation (Surat Keterangan Pindah) and other supporting documents can be used in line with applicable import duty and tax exemption requirements. Make sure what you're shipping matches and meets the requirements stated in your submitted documents. For the full details on requirements and documents, see [the complete customs guide for personal belongings](/articles/indonesia-customs-guide-personal-belongings).

### 5. Force Majeure

Some conditions are beyond the control of both the sender and the logistics provider. Extreme weather, natural disasters, transportation disruptions, and certain cross-border situations can all lead to changes in shipping schedules.

These conditions are dynamic and can occur in either the origin country or Indonesia. If a disruption happens, logistics providers will typically share updates on the situation and the latest time estimate.

## Tips for Planning Your Shipment

Planning your timing well can help you avoid delays and keep your move running smoothly. Before locking in a shipping schedule, keep the following in mind:

### 1. Pick the Right Shipping Date

Avoid scheduling pickup or shipping around weekends and public holidays, in either the origin country or Indonesia. During these periods, logistics providers and related authorities may operate on adjusted schedules.

It's worth checking the holiday calendar in both countries and choosing a shipping date that falls on a business day, so pickup, shipping, and customs clearance can all run as smoothly as possible.

### 2. Schedule Pickup Well Before Your Departure Date

Don't wait until close to your moving-out date or flight to Indonesia to ship your belongings. Give yourself enough time to prepare your goods and account for any possible schedule changes or issues along the way.

Ideally, start packing around 14 days before departure, and book your pickup somewhere between 10 and 5 days out. That way, your goods are ready for pickup well before you leave the origin country, with some buffer time in case anything comes up. To make sure nothing gets missed while packing, you can follow [a checklist for moving back to Indonesia](/articles/moving-back-to-indonesia-checklist).

### 3. Finalize Your Shipment Details Before Pickup

Before your goods are picked up, make sure the contents, quantity, weight, destination address, and recipient details are all accurate and finalized.

Changing information after your shipment is already on its way can affect the administrative and customs clearance process, especially if there's a mismatch between what was declared and the actual contents. Make sure everything has been checked and confirmed before shipping begins.

### 4. Factor In Customs Clearance Time

Don't just count the transit time from the origin country to Indonesia. Once your goods arrive, they still need to go through customs clearance before being forwarded to their destination.

Since inspection times vary from one shipment to another, it's worth building in buffer time so your goods don't arrive right up against your return date or other needs in Indonesia.

### 5. Align Your Shipping Schedule With Your Return Plans

Ideally, your shipping timeline should be built around your flight or moving-out date, not the other way around. Make sure there's enough gap between pickup, estimated shipping time, customs clearance, and your actual return date. That way, you won't need to worry about leaving the origin country before your shipment is processed, or waiting too long for it to arrive once you're already in Indonesia.
