After finishing your studies in Australia, there's a lot to prepare before returning to Indonesia. Besides handling documents, booking flight tickets, and completing university administration, students also need to think about how to bring all their personal belongings home.

Over time, the things you've accumulated while living in Australia inevitably grow — from clothes and textbooks to household items and electronics. When your studies end, bringing all these items as flight luggage often becomes impractical and can add extra costs due to baggage capacity limits.

So, what needs to be prepared to make the moving process go smoothly?

## 1. Choose What to Bring

Before you start packing, take the time to sort your belongings by priority. After living in Australia for several years, the amount of stuff you own is certainly far greater than when you first arrived.

Start by separating items you'll actually use again in Indonesia, such as clothes, books, electronics, or other personal necessities. Items you no longer need can be sold, given to friends, or donated so they don't add to your shipping load.

However, don't decide too hastily. During your studies, there may be many beloved items that accompanied your daily life — from your favorite mug that kept you company during late-night assignments, the jacket you wore through your first winter, a notebook full of notes, to small decorations that made your room feel like home.

They may not be worth much, but each item holds a story and is part of your college journey in Australia. Meanwhile, items you no longer need — especially larger ones — can be sold to friends, offered at a *garage sale*, or donated.

By sorting your belongings early on, the packing process becomes more organized. You'll also be able to estimate shipping volume more accurately, making costs more efficient.

## 2. Prepare the Required Documents

Before shipping your belongings to Indonesia, make sure all documents are ready. Complete documentation will make it easier to process the **Surat Keterangan Pindah (SKP)** (Certificate of Relocation) and help ensure a smooth inspection by Customs when your goods arrive in Indonesia.

Some documents generally required include:

- Surat Keterangan Pindah (SKP) from the Indonesian Embassy/Consulate (KBRI/KJRI) in your country of residence.
- Passport and Indonesian ID card (KTP).
- Proof of return, such as your *flight ticket* and *boarding pass*.
- Supporting study documents, such as your *Letter of Acceptance (LoA)* and diploma or certificate of graduation.
- Other supporting documents as required by Customs.

The SKP is a document listing the relocation items eligible for import duty and tax exemption *(tax exemption)*. This facility applies to items listed in the SKP and shipped within the period of **90 days before to 90 days after (H-90 to H+90)** the owner's arrival in Indonesia.

Before applying for the SKP, make sure you registered upon arrival in Australia and reported your return to the local KBRI or KJRI. Both steps are required for the SKP to be issued. With complete documents from the start, the *customs clearance* process in Indonesia can run more smoothly.

## 3. Choose How to Bring Your Belongings

When returning to Indonesia, students generally have two options for bringing their belongings: flight luggage or cargo services. The best choice depends on the quantity, size, and type of items being brought.

### Bringing Items via Flight Luggage

If the items you're bringing are limited to clothes, study documents, or personal necessities in small quantities, flight luggage can be a practical option. However, keep in mind that every airline has weight and size limits for luggage. Exceeding these limits will incur additional baggage fees, which can be quite substantial, especially for international flights.

### Using Cargo Services

Meanwhile, if you've accumulated a lot of belongings during your studies — such as several boxes of clothes, books, kitchenware, electronics, or dorm equipment — using a cargo service will be more practical and efficient. Your items will be shipped separately from your flight, so you won't need to carry multiple suitcases when you return and can travel more comfortably.

For students returning after several years of study, cargo services are also a solution for shipping large quantities of belongings without worrying about flight baggage limits.

## 4. Pack Your Belongings Properly

Proper packing plays an important role in keeping your items in good condition during international shipping. This is especially true if you're bringing fragile items, electronics, or items with sentimental value — secure packaging can help minimize the risk of damage during transit.

To keep your items protected, here are a few things to pay attention to when packing:

- Use sturdy boxes that match the size of your items.
- Wrap fragile items with bubble wrap, foam, or other protective materials, both inside and outside the packaging.
- Avoid empty space inside boxes by filling gaps with paper, foam, or other protective materials so items don't shift during shipping.
- Group items by category to make unpacking in Indonesia easier.
- Label each box so its contents are easier to identify.

In addition, don't forget to document the condition of your items during packing. Take photos of items before packing, after they're arranged in the box before it's closed, and after the box is closed and ready to ship. This documentation can serve as evidence if needed for a Customs inspection or for filing a claim with the logistics provider in case of damage during shipping.

## 5. Determine the Shipping Time

Determining the shipping time is just as important as preparing your items and documents. Before setting a pickup schedule, make sure you've mapped out all your plans before returning, such as finishing coursework, work, or trips to other cities. This is important so that all items to be shipped are truly ready when picked up.

Ideally, shipping and pickup preparations should be done around **14 days before returning to Indonesia**. This timeframe allows room for any changes to the pickup schedule, delays during shipping, or physical inspection by Customs. With careful planning, the moving process can run more smoothly, and your items have a better chance of arriving in Indonesia close to your own arrival time.

## 6. Common Challenges When Moving

### Shipping Taking Longer Than Estimated

International shipping doesn't always go directly from Australia to Indonesia. In some cases, items may transit through another country before reaching Indonesia. In addition, the pickup process, flight schedules, international courier operations, and Customs inspections can also affect shipping time. Therefore, it's best to plan your shipment well ahead of your return date so you have buffer time in case of delays along the way.

### Unaccounted Costs

Many students only estimate shipping costs, when there are actually other expenses to prepare for. If you choose to bring items as flight luggage *(hand carry)*, you may be charged additional baggage fees if you exceed the airline's limit. Meanwhile, if using a cargo service, there are several costs to consider, such as extra packing needs, *warehouse fee*, or other support services. By understanding all cost components from the start, you can choose the shipping method that best fits your needs and budget.

### Customs Inspection

All relocation items from abroad will go through a Customs inspection upon arrival in Indonesia. The inspection of goods and documents proceeds according to the arrival queue, so processing times can vary between shipments. To help speed up the *customs clearance* process, make sure all documents are complete and every item is accurately declared according to its contents. Complete documentation and accurate information will minimize issues during inspection.

## 7. Tips to Make Moving Easier

To make the process of moving from Australia to Indonesia go more smoothly, here are some tips you can follow:

- **Plan your return well in advance.** Set your return date and item pickup time so they don't conflict with your coursework, work, or other trips.
- **Prepare all documents early.** Make sure documents such as the SKP, passport, KTP, proof of return, and supporting study documents are complete before shipping.
- **Pack your items securely.** Use sturdy boxes, extra protection for fragile items, and document the condition of items before shipping.
- **Choose the right shipping method.** Decide between flight luggage or cargo services based on the quantity and type of items you're bringing.
- **Consult your shipping needs with a cargo provider.** By consulting early, you can get information on pickup schedules, required documents, and estimated shipping times, making your return preparations more organized.
