Deciding to move back and actually starting the process of moving from the Netherlands to Indonesia is more than just booking a flight. There's customs paperwork, import duty conditions, and the household items that have been with you for years in the land of windmills. Without the right preparation, this process can take longer than expected and lead to unforeseen costs at the port or airport of arrival.

This article covers what you need to know before moving from the Netherlands to Indonesia — from the latest legal basis, to the documents you'll need from the Indonesian Embassy in The Hague, to the customs inspection stages, and which items don't qualify for duty exemption.

## What Counts as "Relocation Goods," and Who Qualifies for Duty Exemption?

In customs terms, relocation goods (*barang pindahan*) are household items belonging to someone who used to live abroad and is now moving to Indonesia. This facility is generally aimed at Indonesian citizens returning after working, studying, or living abroad, as well as foreign nationals relocating to Indonesia for work.

Important to understand: duty exemption isn't an automatic right. You still need to meet every requirement set by the customs authority, and customs officers retain the authority to conduct a physical inspection of your belongings even when your documents are complete.

## The Latest Legal Basis You Should Know

Relocation goods used to be regulated under PMK No. 28/PMK.04/2008, but this has since been replaced by Minister of Finance Regulation (PMK) No. 25 of 2025 on Customs Provisions for the Import of Relocation Goods, which took effect at the end of June 2025. The update was meant to provide legal certainty and consistent treatment for both Indonesian citizens returning home and foreign nationals relocating here.

A few core principles apply:

- Relocation goods should ideally arrive together with their owner, or within a specific window of time before or after the owner's arrival.
- Goods must originate from your previous country of domicile — in your case, the Netherlands.
- The facility applies to household items for personal use, not trade goods.

## Documents You'll Need for Moving From the Netherlands to Indonesia

### 1. Certificate of Relocation from the Indonesian Embassy in The Hague

This is the core document that your duty exemption application is built on. It's issued by the Indonesian Representative in your country of domicile — for those based in the Netherlands, that's the Indonesian Embassy in The Hague. Issuing this certificate is generally free of charge, though processing time varies depending on how complete your supporting documents are.

### 2. Supporting Documents Based on Your Reason for Returning

Required documents vary depending on your status:

- **Workers:** employment contract or a letter confirming the end of your employment.
- **Students:** a certificate of graduation or completion of studies.
- **Other categories** (following a spouse, family reasons, etc.): documentation explaining your reason for having lived abroad.

### 3. Customs Documents

Once your goods arrive in Indonesia, the importer (or their representative) needs to submit a Special Goods Import Declaration (PIBK), attaching supporting documents such as a packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, a copy of your passport, and a detailed list of item types, quantities, and estimated value.

## The Customs Process for Relocation Goods, Step by Step

1. **Before departure** — Apply for your Certificate of Relocation at the Indonesian Embassy in The Hague, along with the required supporting documents.
2. **Shipping your goods** — Goods are shipped by sea or air, ideally within a timeframe that lines up with your own arrival.
3. **Arrival in Indonesia** — Submit the PIBK to the customs office where your goods enter.
4. **Physical inspection** — Customs officers are authorized to inspect your goods to confirm they match your documents and meet the applicable requirements.
5. **Issuance of SPPB** — Once every requirement is met and no prohibited or restricted items are found, customs issues a Goods Release Approval Letter (SPPB), which is what allows you to collect your shipment.

## Items That Don't Qualify for Duty Exemption

Not everything automatically qualifies. A few categories are generally excluded:

- Motor vehicles (land, water, or air)
- Goods classified as trade merchandise rather than personal use
- Goods subject to prohibition or restriction rules (lartas)

For these categories, there's still a shipping pathway available, just through a different duty and documentation mechanism than the relocation goods scheme.

## Practical Tips for Planning Your Move From the Netherlands to Indonesia

- **Start your Embassy paperwork early.** Legalization and document verification take time, especially if you apply close to your departure date.
- **Make your packing list as detailed as possible.** The more thorough your list, the smoother your inspection at the port or airport.
- **Separate personal items from anything that could be flagged as restricted or high-value**, so you don't run into extra questions during inspection.
- **Discuss your shipping timeline with your moving provider**, since your goods ideally arrive close to your own arrival date in Indonesia.
- **Keep copies of every document** (Embassy certificate, passport, boarding pass, packing list) in both physical and digital form.
