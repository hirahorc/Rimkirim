# Expat Relocation order flow: handoff

A working prototype of the epic *Revamp of the Expat Relocation Customer Web Experience*, built on dummy data with no backend. This page tells dev what is real, what is a stand-in to replace, and where the prototype departs from the epic. Terms follow [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md); the two architecture decisions are [ADR 0001](adr/0001-expat-order-flow-apart-from-pesan.md) and [ADR 0002](adr/0002-customs-path-recommended-by-ops.md).

To see the open questions in context, add `?review=1` to any page of the flow.

## The flow

| Step | Route | Component |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check Rate + Select Rate | `/expat-relocation/rates` | `components/expat-order/RatesView.tsx` |
| The plan: Eligibility Check, Personal Information, Packages | `/expat-relocation/plan` | `PlanView.tsx` → `EligibilityStep.tsx`, `DetailsStep.tsx`, `PackingStep.tsx` |
| Booking Summary | `/expat-relocation/booking/[number]` | `BookingView.tsx` |
| Internal ticket + email preview | `/simulator`, tab "Pesanan expat" | `components/tracking/ExpatBookingsTab.tsx` |

The plan is one page: its three parts stand down a single sheet, one open at a time. A finished part folds to a line that says it back and the next opens under it; any part reopens in place ("Edit"), and a part doesn't open until the ones above it are in. There is no stepper and no step in the URL (an old `?step=` link simply opens the plan). One action runs the page, with the estimate beside it: Continue until the last part is in, then Confirm application. It sits under the Selected Service on a wide screen and on a bar at the foot of a phone. Check Rate comes before the plan and isn't one of its parts; from the plan, the way back to it is Back, under the parts. The service is the one thing of Check Rate's that changes on the plan: "Change" on the Selected Service opens Check Rate's own rates in a dialog (a sheet on a phone), and picking one keeps every answer; the origin country and the weight still change on Check Rate only. The structure was chosen from a prototype of three (the plan as one page, one question per screen, or a checklist in any order); the one page won for keeping the estimate and one action in reach on a phone.

The draft lives in `lib/store/useExpatOrderStore.ts` (localStorage key `rimkirim:expat-order`) and survives reloads until it's submitted. Arriving at `/plan` without a Selected Rate sends the customer back to Check Rate; otherwise the plan opens on the first part still to do (the questions, then Personal Information, then the Packages), wherever the draft had reached. Changing the origin country or the weight clears the Selected Rate; changing a side's Address (on Check Rate, or in its card) clears the pin set for the old one, and from Check Rate its Detail Address too, with a notice on the destination's card, whose detail is required.

## Breaking it with real data

In local development every page of the flow carries a small data toggle (`components/expat-order/dev/`, the break-ui skill): **Demo data**, **Worst case** (the longest values each field accepts, an email that can't wrap, sizes typed in the wrong unit), **Empty**, **One**, and **40 packages**. It swaps the stored draft and its booking, so the screens render the data untouched; `?data=worst` on any flow URL does the same, and **Off** puts your own draft back. It never renders outside development. Keep the worst-case dataset as the check for the next change to these screens.

## Every state on a canvas

`/canvas` lays the flow out as boards, the way a design file would: one per page (the landing, Check Rate, the plan, the Booking Summary, the internal ticket) and an overview of the happy path. A board holds every state its stories ask for as frames, at desktop and at phone width. A lane is a ticket of the brief (RPW-281 to RPW-297); its rows are the positive path, the negative paths and the edge data; an arrow runs from a state to the one an action leads to, within its lane. A state the brief asks for that isn't built stands as a dashed placeholder.

The board is **kanvas** (`../kanvas`, Hira's own product, kept in its own repository): each frame is a static snapshot of the page in its state, its HTML and CSS frozen with its pictures, so a board of a hundred frames opens light and nothing in a frame moves or scrolls. A frame is a whole page, one screen (for what's pinned to it: a dialog, a sheet, a toast), or, on the landing, one whole section. Since 2026-11-25 it replaces the canvas whose frames were the live pages in iframes (it took gigabytes of memory). The board's site is `public/canvas`, committed and served as files; `/canvas` and the old `/canvas/<board>` links (with the scene they pointed at) redirect to it (`next.config.ts`).

Clicking a frame opens its drawer: its kind and ticket, Copy image (the picture with a line under it: ticket, state, screen size, the day it was copied), Copy link, Open live, how it's reached, what it shows, its rules, and its **changelog**. A lane's drawer has its story, where the prototype departs from the brief and what the team still has to confirm (the review notes of `?review=1`, quoted), its changelog, and Copy lane as a picture. Comments still go on the Jira tickets: the board is a map.

**Open live** opens the prototype in the scene's state: the page with `?scene=<id>`. Scene mode gives the page storage of its own, in memory and filled with the scene's seed, before any store reads it, then plays the scene's steps (`lib/canvas/boot.ts`, an inline script in `app/layout.tsx`, and `SceneRunner`); the page says it's a scene, with the way back to its frame on the board and the way out to the real page. On a page that has a board, the switch at the foot of the screen, "Prototype | Canvas", opens that board, and the board's bar links to the page. Scene mode and the switch ship wherever `HAS_CANVAS` (`lib/env.ts`) says, production included; the board's pages say noindex.

**The board follows the code, commit by commit.** After a change to the flow is committed to main, with the app running:

```sh
npm run kanvas -- snap     # every scene at both widths, frozen into public/canvas
npm run kanvas -- log      # changelog entries for what looks different, from the commits since
npm run kanvas -- build    # the board's viewer beside the frames
git add public/canvas && git commit -m "chore(kanvas): frames for …"
```

A frame counts as changed when its words changed (copy, however small) or its picture looks different; a frame whose page is the same keeps its files, so a snapshot of nothing new changes nothing. A change across most of a board is one entry on the board. A commit says who asked with a `Source:` trailer; otherwise the tickets it names (RPW-293) are its source. `npm run kanvas -- check` opens every scene and plays its steps, and fails when one can't be reached. `npm run kanvas -- view` opens the board on this machine, where Open live opens the scene in a real browser window. `npm run kanvas` runs kanvas from `../kanvas` (built with `npm run build` there); it isn't a dependency of the app, so nothing of it reaches the deployment but the files in `public/canvas`.

For whoever adds to it:

- The scenes stay in `lib/canvas/expat/scenes.ts` (their seeds, steps, lanes and tickets, built on the demo order of the datasets above), one source for the frames and for Open live. `kanvas.config.ts` only says them the way kanvas reads them; it imports nothing from kanvas, so the app builds without it.
- Steps find what they press by the flow's own copy, so a reworded label is still found. A moment that would pass (a toast, "Checking…") stays because the timer that ends it is held back.
- Scene titles, notes and arrow labels are in English, as the brief and its tickets are: they travel into the copied pictures.
- Every scene runs at the board's clock (`clock` in `kanvas.config.ts`): dates on the frames are worked out from it, so they don't change with the day. Moving it changes every frame with a date, as one entry on each board.
- Another flow adds a folder beside `lib/canvas/expat` and joins `lib/canvas/registry.ts`; kanvas.config.ts reads every board there.

## Feedback

What the flow says back is in DESIGN.md (The expat order flow, "The plan's one action" and "Feedback"). For dev:

- Toasts are Sonner, through the app's one `<Toaster />`; each call that can fire from an effect carries an `id`, so it's said once. They fire in `PlanView.tsx` (what the action waits on, the plan opened without a rate, a service changed, a failed confirm) and `visit.ts` (answers kept from an earlier visit).
- `visit.ts` keeps two facts for the visit in `sessionStorage`, neither of which the order depends on: that the kept-answers toast was settled, and the booking Confirm application just made (so its Booking Summary draws its check once).
- The plan's action finds what's missing in the DOM: a part marks it with `aria-invalid="true"` (a field) or `data-missing` (an answer group, the date field). A new required thing only needs one of those marks to be counted and focused.
- `pace.ts` holds the flow's deliberate floors (Check rate, Confirm application, the estimate settling after the packages change; DESIGN.md, Motion, Pace). Each is `paced(work, floor)`: the work and the floor run together and the longer wins. Today the work is local, so the floor is the whole wait; when the rates and the booking are requests, pass the request as `work` and leave the floor in.
- **Still to build, once the real services are in** (today the rates and the address suggestions are local and instant, so there's nothing to wait on or fail): a loading state on "Check rate" and on the address search, a failed-request message with a way to try again, "no service for this route", and a failed Confirm application that says what the server said (the toast for it is there; `submit()` can only fail on an incomplete draft today).

## The Customs Category rule (dummy, for ops to confirm)

One pure function, `customsCategory()` in `lib/order/expat-order.ts`, read top down, first match wins:

| # | When | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the reason is Something else | `personal_shipment` |
| 2 | work or study, but no ITAS for work or study of 12 months or more yet (Q4 "No or not yet") | `needs_review` |
| 3 | the goods don't ship from the country they've been living in (Q5 "No") | `needs_review` |
| 4 | already in Indonesia, whenever they arrived | `needs_review` |
| 5 | the rest: work or study, abroad, with that ITAS, the goods from where they live | `eligible_pindahan` |

Rows 2 and 3 are the prototype's, written when the PM's revised questions were caught up (2026-11-24): they replace the visa rows (no visa; a visa but neither a VITAS nor an ITAS), since relocation goods need that ITAS and goods from the country the customer lived in. The rule the staging build runs is the dev team's; this table is for ops to confirm. Q1 (what's shipping) and the arrival date never move the flag; ops weighs them. The flag is never rendered to the customer, on the Booking Summary or in the email. It shows only on the internal ticket. At review ops recommends the Customs Path that suits the booking best, then asks for its documents, and the customer decides (ADR 0002). No documents have been sent at that point, so the review checks the booking, not documents.

## Where the prototype departs from the epic

- **Final price.** The epic (section 5) says the final price is set at quotation. The team corrected this: the Quotation is still an estimate, and the final price comes on the Invoice, once the packages reach Rimkirim's warehouse and are measured and weighed again. All copy follows the correction. The order after pickup, from the team: the packages are shipped to Indonesia; they enter the 3PL's warehouse, where the 3PL measures them again; customs; once cleared, the 3PL invoices Rimkirim; they go on to Rimkirim's warehouse, where Rimkirim measures them again; Rimkirim sends the customer the Invoice; the customer pays; Rimkirim delivers to the destination Address. So the Invoice never comes at pickup and delivery follows payment, and the Booking Summary's "What happens next" stops at pickup (review, quotation, pickup), with one line under the steps, apart from the pickup, saying the invoice comes once the belongings have arrived at Rimkirim's warehouse in Indonesia. The flow never offers that weighing as a reason to guess, though: the customer's own measurements are still asked for, as their record (the team's call). So a rough figure is "fine for now" (the weight on Check Rate, a package not packed yet), the details can be updated after the team's review, and the lines about the final price name the invoice only.
- **Detail Addresses, in the cards, and no map pin.** The epic asks for a Detail Address field on both sides, each "(required)", pinned on a map. Personal Information has no such field and no map: each side is a card (where from, where to) with a "Change" that turns the card into its own editor on the same page, holding the Address (searched again, inside its country) and the Detail Address. Both Detail Addresses are optional, the team's call, where the epic marks each "(required)": a card without one offers it ("Add detail address", said to be optional), and the plan goes on without it. The team dropped the pin (the map was a stand-in, and the detail carries the unit and landmarks); a pin on an older draft or booking is ignored. Changing an Address here never moves the rate in the prototype (the rate is priced on the origin country and the weight); if the real rate reads the postal code, an Address changed here must reprice. The origin country itself changes on Check Rate only.
- **Required fields.** The epic marks only the Detail Addresses "(required)". Here it's the other way round: the Detail Addresses are optional (above), and the name, email, phone and the answer to when to collect are required, since the email and phone are needed for the confirmation and the follow-up. The Pickup Date is required only within 3 months (below).
- **Pickup Date.** The epic (section 7) opens the calendar three days out. The team moved it to tomorrow; weekends stay closed. Revised by the PM (RPW-293, 2026-11-23): Your details first asks "When would you like us to collect your shipment?", with nothing chosen, as two cards: "Within 3 months" ("We'll prepare your quotation after our team reviews it.") and "More than 3 months" ("We'll get in touch closer to the time."). There's no calendar until it's answered. Within 3 months, the calendar runs from tomorrow to day 90 (today is day 0) and a date is required ("Pickup date", "Any weekday from tomorrow to {date}", naming the last weekday, where the PM wrote "Any date up to {date}", since weekends are closed); more than 3 months, it runs from day 91 with no end and the date is optional ("Pickup date (optional)", the word in the label), and an empty one is a finished answer, never shown as a problem. Three months is exactly 90 days, so the two ranges neither touch nor leave a gap, and the customer never sees a day count. The day of the order and weekends are never open, the PM's answers the same day. Changing the answer clears a date picked for the other ("We cleared {date} because it's outside the dates this option allows.", said as a status). The calendar opens on the first day that can be picked, with focus on it; a day that can't be picked is aria-disabled and still reachable by the arrows; the day in hand wears a solid Ink ring; on a phone the calendar is the house's bottom sheet (the Eligibility Check's two dates too). A booking more than 3 months out without a date says "To be confirmed, more than 3 months from now" on the Booking Summary and its email, and What happens next's pickup step says the date will be agreed closer to the time. Drafts saved before this take the answer their date falls in (store version 3).
- **Packages.** Not in the epic. The team added it as the plan's last part, after Personal Information (it first sat before it), with the fields of the WNI flow's Items & Packages module: the values' currency first (the origin country's until it's changed; it sat under the packages, and the team moved it up, since every value typed in a package wears it), then per package, its packaging (Others asks what it's packed in, as free text), the weight and three sides, and each item's description, quantity and value. Weight, sides, description and quantity are required, and so is what it's packed in when the packaging is Others; values are optional. The module's measurement photos, and its warning on confirming without them, are left out, the team's call. The booking carries it in the shared Packing List shape (`ItemsData` in `lib/types/packing.ts`), and the Booking Summary, the email and the ticket show it. Once it's measured, the Selected Service's weight and estimate are the packages' (see Stand-ins). Revised 2026-11-20, the team's call: each package first picks the standard box it's closest to (Box A, B, C) or Other; a standard box asks no weight or sides and is estimated at its volumetric weight, and only Other asks the packaging, weight and sides. What's inside is a row per category (a searchable list; Others asks what it is), with its quantity and its value in total, in place of a typed description and a value each. Drafts saved before this are kept: a measured package becomes Other, an item becomes Others with its name, and its value each becomes the row's total (store version 2, `migratePacking`). Revised again the same day: the field is "Package size" and its fourth tile "Custom" (Other in the data); every package, once its size is picked, is asked its "Estimated weight", and Custom asks only its packaging and sides. The three sizes are labelled Small Box, Medium Box and Large Box (Box A, B and C in the data; the team's call, 2026-11-20).
- **Changing the service on the plan.** Not in the epic, which has the rate picked once, at Select Rate. The team added "Change" to the Selected Service: it opens every rate for the route (each carrier and service, the Special Rate first, then cheapest first) in Check Rate's own rate card, without leaving the plan, and the rate in hand is marked "Selected". Unlike Select Rate, where the epic shows one rate and keeps the rest behind "See more rates", the list opens whole, since the customer is there to compare. Whether a real rate may be repriced on the packages' weight when the service changes is for ops to confirm (`?review=1` says so in the dialog).
- **The customs guide on the plan.** Not in the epic. The team made "How your belongings clear customs" a button that's always on screen on the plan, wherever the customer is in it (beside the form, under its action, from lg; at the top of the bar on a phone), opening the three Customs Paths in a dialog. It used to sit folded at the foot of the Eligibility Check, and isn't there any more. The first time the plan opens, the guide opens itself, half a second in, out of that button; closed, it goes back into the button, which rings once, and that first time words beside it say it opens again from there. "First time" is remembered on the device (`localStorage`, `rimkirim:expat-order:customs-seen`) once the guide has been closed. `CustomsGuide.tsx`; the copy is the same `CustomsBody` the Booking Summary folds under "Documents to get ready".
- **Who gets in touch after booking.** Not in the epic. The team asked for it (2026-11-23): once the application is confirmed, the customer experience team contacts the customer to complete what the order still needs, the customs documents (Document Compliance) and the packing list for customs (Create Packing List). The Booking Summary says it in a dialog, "We'll contact you within 1 working day", that opens once, on arriving from Confirm application, after the received head's three beats (at once with reduced motion), and never on a reload or a link back (`ContactDialog.tsx`, on the same `fresh` as the head's check). The facts, from the team: on WhatsApp and email, within 1 working day, before the quotation, and nothing to prepare or send until then, since CX says which documents. It holds for every booking, a pickup more than 3 months out included: CX checks the booking submission then, and "We'll get in touch closer to the time" on that answer is about the pickup and the quotation (the team, 2026-11-23). A dialog is gone once it's closed, so What happens next says the same in step 1, which the email shares; it stays three steps. "Packing list" here is the customs document, the one the Packages step was renamed away from.
- **No footer in the flow.** Not in the epic. The team dropped the site footer from every page past the landing (rates, plan, booking), as the WNI order flow does, so the form is the page. The header's "Need help?" stays the way to a person; the landing keeps its footer (`isExpatOrderFlowRoute`, `AppFooter.tsx`).
- **Cannot be shipped, and a date in both Q2 branches.** From the PM's eligibility rule v1 (2026-11-16). Right under Q1's categories, the flow tells (it doesn't ask) what can't be shipped: motor vehicles, alcohol or tobacco, and anything hazardous or flammable, which none of the three Customs Paths lets in. Each is pictured and struck through, in Ink, with a link to the landing's full "Not allowed" list (`#not-allowed`). The PM's playground has them as answers that stop the check; the team made them information, so nothing is ticked and the order goes on. The landing's list (alcohol, cigarettes and vapes, weapons, flammables, cash, jewellery, plants) took motor vehicles too, and so did the FAQ's "What can't I ship?". Q2 asks "When do you arrive in Indonesia?" and a date either way: the day they plan to land when they haven't arrived yet (from today on), the day they landed when they already live in Indonesia (up to today), in place of the epic's length of stay (less than 3 months to more than 1 year). The Booking Summary and the email give it one line, "Arrival in Indonesia: Planned for …" or "Arrived on …". The date is recorded for ops. The PM's 90-day window between the arrival and the day the goods land doesn't move the flag here; the flag stays the team's dummy rule above, for the dev team to build.
- **Five questions, the visa as one ITAS question.** The PM's revision, built by the dev team straight to staging; the prototype caught up from a screenshot of it (2026-11-24). Why they're moving comes third, "Why are you moving to Indonesia?", answered "Work", "Study" or "Something else" (it was Q4, "What's the primary reason for your relocation?", answered "Working", "Student", "Others"); "Something else" still asks for a few words. The visa question and its two follow-ups ("Do you already have a visa?", then a VITAS and an ITAS) became one, Q4: "Do you hold an ITAS for work or study, valid 12 months or more?" ("An ITAS is the stay permit you get once you're in Indonesia."), answered "Yes" or "No or not yet". A fifth asks "Are these goods being shipped from the country you have been living in?", Yes or No. The lead says five questions. On the Booking Summary, the email and the ticket, the answers read in the questions' order: the reason, "ITAS: Work or study, valid 12 months or more" (or "No or not yet"), "From where you live: Yes". A draft or booking saved before keeps its other answers and loses its visa ones (store version 4). For a draft, Q4 and Q5 are asked: the check opens again with the two of them to answer, and says nothing more (a notice at its head, "Questions 4 and 5 are new. Your other answers are still saved.", was taken out by Hira, 2026-11-24). A booking lists the answers it has: one never given isn't a row, on the Booking Summary, the email or the ticket. The landing's three mentions of the count (How it works, the closing's "After this", the FAQ's "How do I book?") say five too.
- **Q2, Q4 and Q5 in marketing communications' words.** Andita (2026-11-24), over the PM's: Q2 asks "Have you arrived in Indonesia yet?" ("We'll use this to guide you on the right customs requirements."), answered "Not yet" or "Yes, I'm already here", where it read "When do you arrive in Indonesia?", answered "I have not arrived yet" or "I already live in Indonesia"; the date under each answer is unchanged. Q4 reads "Do you hold a limited stay permit (ITAS) for work or study, valid for 12 months or more?" ("An ITAS is the stay permit you receive after arriving in Indonesia."), answered "Yes" or "No or not yet", and Q5 "Are you shipping from the country you've been living in?". The plan's folded line says Q2 back as "Not in Indonesia yet" or "Already in Indonesia", since "Not yet" alone loses its question.
- **Personas (Story 1).** The landing page doesn't frame the work and study personas; they appear only as the reason in Q3. Decided by the team with the PM.
- **Passenger Goods.** The epic puts it out of scope. The service covers it, so the landing and the flow explain all three Customs Paths equally, with no choice for the customer (decided with the PM).

## Copy

All flow copy is in `lib/i18n/messages.ts` under `expatOrder`. The page is English only, like the landing, but the Indonesian keeps the same shape (the `Messages` type enforces it). The epic's question wording is kept verbatim, with three changes: Q1's subtext says "an accurate estimate" where the epic says "quote", since the figure is an Estimate (CONTEXT.md); Q2 asks whether they've arrived (the PM's rule v1, in marketing communications' words since 2026-11-24: "Have you arrived in Indonesia yet?", answered "Not yet" or "Yes, I'm already here"), followed by an "Estimated arrival date" (the day they plan to land; the team's call, 2026-11-20, where it read "Arrival date") or an "Arrival date" (the day they landed), where the epic asks "Are you already in Indonesia, or still abroad?" and then a date or a length of stay; and the Pickup Date is labelled "Pickup date" where the epic asks "When are you planning to relocate" (the team's call: the date is the day we collect, not the day of the move).
